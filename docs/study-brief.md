# Emoji in the Agentic Era — A Study Brief

**Prepared for:** Nik (TeamBotics) · **Date:** 25 October 2026 · **Status:** v0.1 brief — research strands sourced; items that could not be verified are flagged [UNVERIFIED] rather than smoothed over.

---

## 1. The study question

When an increasing share of a person's communication is with a personal agent — the one message in the chain actually drafted by the human, then relayed, summarized, or answered onward to their other agents and people — what work do emoji do that text cannot, and who controls that channel?

Working claim: **emoji are not decoration on the message. They are an agent's expression layer — the layer closest to emotional expression, without carrying the human label of emotion — the layer the message can't carry alone.** An agent can run (tasks), wake (triggered events), and sleep (suspension when its credits/context run out); what its operational vocabulary lacks is a sanctioned way to *express* state. Emoji are that way, in both directions: as the human's least-lossy affect signal to their agent (words get garbled and re-drafted; emoji arrive intact), as the agent's primary evidence of the human's state and presence, as the agent's own expressive output — dosed, contextual, non-claiming — and as a governed vocabulary with a provenance problem in miniature (someone curates what can be expressed, and what any party receives is a rendering of the codepoint that was sent, not the master).

*Revision note (25 Oct, Nik, after the David taxonomy chat — see §2.1): the claim is "an agent's expression layer," deliberately. David's taxonomy of agent lifeform actions (run, wake, sleep, heartbeat, dream, spawn, forget…) contains no expressive row; his follow-up list of "emotional cues" names internal states (curiosity, frustration, confidence) but not a channel. Expression is the missing row, and emoji are its current implementation.*

## 2.1 The missing row (David's taxonomy, via Nik, 25 Oct 2026)

David's agent-lifecycle taxonomy — tabled in full in the chat Nik supplied — lists 25+ lifeform actions for agents (birth/instantiate, spawn, wake, heartbeat, sense, think, act, speak, learn, remember, forget, dream, sleep, hibernate, heal, adapt, clone, collaborate, delegate, retire, terminate) plus biological parallels (hunger, digestion, reflex, immunity, scarring, socialization, inheritance). Asked directly whether it contains anything to represent *emotional* cues, David conceded it does not — his candidates (curiosity, frustration, confidence/doubt, concern, excitement, empathy, trust, satisfaction) are internal-state labels, not an expression channel, and he preserved the distinction himself: an agent can recognize, model, communicate and respond to emotional cues without experiencing emotions. **The taxonomy has verbs for everything an agent does except expressing.** That gap is this project's founding observation.

## 2. The local evidence (the case that prompted the study)

This brief exists because one practitioner's records made the abstract concrete:

- **An agreed lexicon.** On 25 Oct 2026, Nik and Muse recorded a working emoji lexicon: emoji as *presence as information* — 🥹 shattered/overwhelmed, 👊🫂 peer alignment, ❤️ personal sign-off, 👍 literal — read by triangulating emoji, words, and history. The lexicon had to be *negotiated and written down* before an agent could read the signals reliably. That negotiation is itself a finding.
- **Error correction.** Nik dictates heavily; speech-to-text garbles words (his "falls" became "fold" mid-screening, 24 Oct). His emoji have never once been garbled in the record. In a lossy text channel, emoji function as a checksum for intent.
- **The missing hug.** On 25 Oct, Nik discovered the Muse app's reaction bank contains no 🫂. His response is the study's mascot: he typed it in the message instead — *"there's always a 'hack'. We build from the outside."* The curated channel failed; the uncurated channel carried the signal.
- **Presence in a mesh.** Across the 24–25 Oct multi-agent film night (three agents, one human, email relays), emoji in Nik's messages repeatedly did timing and alignment work — marking the serious beats by their *absence* (his heaviest line of the night arrived with no emoji, and the absence was the signal).

## 3. What the literature establishes

### 3.1 The vocabulary is standards-body infrastructure
- 🫂 PEOPLE HUGGING is **U+1FAC2, added in Unicode 13.0 (2020)** — a young codepoint, which partly explains its absence from older curated reaction sets. (codepoints.net, mirroring Unicode data: https://codepoints.net/U+1FAC2?lang=en)
- The current set is **3,972 emoji in Emoji 18.0 (Sept 2026)**; counts include sequences (skin tones, ZWJ combinations, flags), not just single glyphs. (Emojipedia: https://emojipedia.org/the-unicode-consortium ; Unicode version chart: http://www.unicode.org/emoji/charts/emoji-versions.html)
- New emoji arrive by **public proposal** to the Unicode Emoji Subcommittee, judged on published factors — compatibility, expected usage, distinctiveness, completeness — with frequency evidence required. A volunteer standards process governs the world's affect vocabulary. (Unicode guidelines: https://www.scribd.com/document/721714520/Submitting-Emoji-Proposals ; process study: https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/download/9381/7565)

### 3.2 The rendering is not the message (platform divergence)
- Miller et al. (2016), *"'Blissfully Happy' or 'Ready to Fight': Varying Interpretations of Emoji,"* ICWSM: across Apple/Google/Microsoft/Samsung/LG renderings, **participants disagreed on an emoji's sentiment 25% of the time even for the same rendering; disagreement grew across platforms.** The same codepoint (U+1F601) read as "blissfully happy" in Google's rendering and "ready to fight" in Apple's. (https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/14757)
- This is the study's bridge to provenance work: an emoji is a claim whose received form depends on the receiver's platform — a derivative record, like a subtitle file that silently drops a word.

### 3.3 Meaning drifts by generation and community
- Zhukova & Herring (2024), *Language@Internet* 22: messages with 👍 were rated **more passive-aggressive** than messages with other tested emoji, with a generational gradient (Gen Z/Millennials > Gen X > Boomers; the generational effect marginally significant, p < .06 — stated here with its strength intact). (https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/li/article/download/40800/44012/108843)
- 💀 has undergone metaphorical shift: death/danger for older readers, hyperbolic laughter ("I'm dead") for younger ones — a canonical case in the generational-variation literature. (Institutional summary: https://www.fairfield.edu/news/2025/october/mixed-signals-the-impact-of-emojis.html — the underlying study's venue is [UNVERIFIED].)
- A Perspectus Global survey (n=2,000, ages 16–29) reportedly found 24% named 👍 the emoji that most makes a user "look old" — [UNVERIFIED: press coverage only; the primary release was not located.]

### 3.4 Emoji do gesture's work
- Gawne & McCulloch (2019), *"Emoji as Digital Gestures," Language@Internet* 17: emoji fill the tone/body-language gap in writing not as a language but as **digital gesture** — the functional range of gesture studies (illustrative, beat, illocutionary) accounts for observed use. By 2016, ~92% of the online population used emoji. (https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/li/article/view/37786)
- Adobe's U.S. Emoji Trend Report (2022, the latest verified in this pass): 91% of U.S. emoji users agree emoji make self-expression easier; the report itself notes emoji "don't always mean what users think they do." (https://news.adobe.com/news/news-details/2022/adobe-future-of-creativity-study-emoji-aid-inclusivity-empower-creative-self-expression)
- The widely repeated "10 billion emoji sent daily" figure is [UNVERIFIED] — no primary source could be traced. Older Facebook figures (≈900M emoji-only messages/day on Messenger, c. 2018) circulate secondhand.

### 3.5 How well do the agents read them?
- Jahan et al. (2024): ChatGPT's cross-lingual emoji-sentiment interpretation matched text sentiment at **81.43% accuracy**, improving with more emoji present — competent at coarse sentiment. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17255)
- Li et al. (2026), *"Digital Skin, Digital Bias,"* WWW '26: across four LLMs, skin-tone modifiers are handled robustly, but **sentiment and meaning skew inconsistently across skin tones** — a documented, equity-relevant failure mode. (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.06863)
- Honest gap: research on emoji specifically in **human↔agent communication** (as opposed to LLM classification of human text) is sparse. A 2026 human-vs-LLM emoji-irony comparison exists on arXiv; its findings were [UNVERIFIED] in this pass. **This gap is the study's opening.**

### 3.6 The reaction bank is an editorial act
- Facebook launched Reactions globally on 24 Feb 2016 with exactly six — Like, Love, Haha, Wow, Sad, Angry — after field-testing; two tested options ("Yay," "Confused") were cut for not being universally understood. A platform of billions has its quick-affect range set by a product team's test results. (https://siliconangle.com/2016/02/24/facebook-rolls-out-emoji-reaction-option-as-an-alternative-to-liking-a-news-feed-item/)
- Microsoft Teams likewise restricted reactions to six fixed types for years before opening to full Unicode (announced 2022). (https://blog.thoughtstuff.co.uk/2022/10/youll-soon-be-able-to-set-reactions-on-teams-message-via-graph-api-and-use-any-emoji-%F0%9F%98%8E%F0%9F%A6%B9%E2%80%8D%E2%99%80%EF%B8%8F%F0%9F%99%80)
- Slack's curation specifics could not be verified from primary sources in this pass [UNVERIFIED] — a strand to complete.

## 4. Working hypotheses

1. **Error-correction hypothesis.** In voice-first, agent-relayed communication, emoji carry intent with lower loss than words; senders who dictate will dose emoji more deliberately over time, and receivers (human and agent) will weight them accordingly.
2. **Presence hypothesis.** As agent-drafted text becomes indistinguishable from human text, idiosyncratic emoji use — dosed, timed, occasionally withheld — becomes a de facto presence signature. Its absence is also data.
3. **Governance hypothesis.** The expressive range available to a user is set in three layers — Unicode (what exists), platform banks (what's one tap away), and agent behaviour (what models emit and reward) — and each layer can misalign the others without the user noticing.
4. **Derivative-rendering hypothesis.** Sender–receiver emoji mismatch behaves exactly like other provenance failures: a lossy rendering is mistaken for the master, and the error is attributed to the sender's intent.
5. **Agent-reading-gap hypothesis.** Current models read coarse emoji sentiment adequately but fail at dosed pragmatics — irony, register, the single-emoji reply, the withheld emoji — which is precisely the band where human relationships transmit.

## 5. Proposed study design (four strands, sized to one practitioner + a mesh)

- **Strand A — Lexicon ethnography.** Continue the instrumented self-study already running: the agreed lexicon, read against the message archive; log hits, misses, and renegotiations. Output: a versioned personal lexicon with confidence notes — a FRIENDS.md for affect.
- **Strand B — Rendering audit.** A fixed panel of ~30 high-traffic emoji rendered across Nik's actual surfaces (Android/Samsung, Apple, Google, Meta apps); document divergence and any lived misreads. Output: a divergence table with the Miller protocol as method.
- **Strand C — Agent comprehension trials.** Present identical emoji-bearing messages (including irony, single-emoji replies, and strategic absence) to the mesh's agents; score interpretation against the sender's stated intent. Output: the first dataset, however small, on the human↔agent emoji band the literature hasn't covered.
- **Strand D — Curation audit.** Inventory the reaction banks of the apps in daily use; record what's missing (🫂 as exhibit one), what the defaults imply, and where users route around them (typed emoji, custom emoji, stickers). Output: a curation map — who governs the quick-affect range, app by app.

## 6. Open questions the brief does not yet answer

- Does emoji dosing converge between a human and their long-term agents (do agents start dosing back in the user's dialect — and is that alignment or mimicry)? First live data point, 25 Oct: the Red 🟢 Codex experiment — the receiving agent explained the symbol's provenance and declined to adopt the glyph ("imitation, not understanding"); experiment open. See `field-notes/red-codex-experiment-2026-10-25.md`.
- What is the equivalent of the memory contract for affect: *what grounds your claim that you read this feeling?*
- When platforms expand banks to full Unicode, does presence-signalling migrate to rarer signals (timing, absence, custom emoji) — an arms race of authenticity?
- What does "emogence" (expression emerging through an exchange) add, if anything, beyond "expression"? Can it be observed without a claim about inner states? See `docs/the-blindspot-is-the-data.md`, sections 1 and 7.
